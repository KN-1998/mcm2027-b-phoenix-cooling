Heat-Related 911 Calls  
      
Phoenix Fire responded to 39 heat-related calls over the reporting period, the lowest weekly 
total reported since late May. More than 50% of calls were for adults aged 55+, a shift from the 
pattern seen in recent weeks that favored younger age groups. The spatial pattern of calls was 
generally similar to prior weeks, with the highest call density in the vicinity of Downtown 
Phoenix. Maryvale and areas east of Downtown Phoenix saw a notable weekly decline in calls.   
Patient Location and Transport Information:  
• Most common locations: roadways (16), single-family and private residences (8).  
• 29 patients (74%) were transported for additional care, including 10 to Banner 
University.  
• Cold water immersion was deployed 3 times for heat stroke patients (season total: 48).  
Heat-Related Deaths 
• 18 heat-related deaths have been confirmed in Maricopa County as of 7/4, with 11 of 
those cases occurring in Phoenix.  
• 215 cases were under investigation in Maricopa County as of 7/4, compared to 173 at 
the same time last year. 
• Of the cases reported countywide to date, 87% occurred outdoors, 67% involved 
substances, and 28% were among people experiencing homelessness.  
• Heat-related death data are updated on the Maricopa County Department of Public 
Health (MCDPH) Heat-Related Illness & Death Surveillance Dashboard

City Program Reports   
• Communications: Posted 11 heat response social media messages that generated 4,063 
impressions and 88 engagements.  
• Homeless Solutions: OHS Street Outreach staff engaged 128 individuals, and distributed 
water and heat relief supplies as needed. Additional heat-related street outreach activities 
are conducted by contractual partners. 
• Innovation: The equivalent of 11,777 single-use water bottles was distributed through 12 
operating chilled drinking fountains; two additional fountains are undergoing routine service.  
• Library: Received positive feedback from a patron who shared how the resources available 
at Cholla Library helped her end her homelessness and find stable housing.  
• Neighborhood Services: Nine new cooling ordinance cases were opened, seven open cases 
were positively resolved, and five air conditioner replacements were completed.
