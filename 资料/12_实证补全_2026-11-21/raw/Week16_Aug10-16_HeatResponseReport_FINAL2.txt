Heat-Related 911 Calls  
      
Phoenix Fire responded to 69 heat-related calls, marking the second consecutive week of 
significant declines relative to the week prior. Areas with higher call volumes remained 
consistent with seasonal patterns, including central city, the I-17 corridor extending north to 
Dunlap Ave., and portions of west Phoenix.  
Patient Location and Transport Information:  
• Most common locations: roadways (20), other business (12), single-family and private 
residences (9).  
• 45 patients (65%) were transported for additional care, including 10 to Banner-University 
and 8 to St. Joe’s.  
• Cold water immersion was used 7 times for heat stroke patients (season total: 186).  
Heat-Related Deaths 
• 81 heat-related deaths have been confirmed in Maricopa County as of 8/15, with 37 of 
those cases occurring in Phoenix.  
• 587 cases were under investigation in Maricopa County as of 8/15, compared to 376 at 
the same time last year. 
• Of the cases reported countywide to date, 68% occurred outdoors, 52% involved 
substances, and 30% were among people experiencing homelessness.  
• Heat-related death data are updated on the Maricopa County Department of Public 
Health (MCDPH) Heat-Related Illness & Death Surveillance Dashboard

City Program Reports 
• Homeless Solutions: OHS Street Outreach staff engaged 374 individuals, and distributed 
water and heat relief supplies as needed. Additional heat-related street outreach activities 
are conducted by contractual partners. OHS staff also recently played a critical role in 
reconnecting a missing person with family through the 20 West Jackson heat respite center.  
• Library: Helped connect a resident using the Cholla extended hour cooling center to the 
Neighborhood Services Department for air conditioning repair assistance.  
• Neighborhood Services: 12 new cooling ordinance cases were opened, 16 open cases were 
resolved with positive outcomes, and 5 new air condition replacements were completed.  
• Trailhead Outreach: CERT volunteers distributed 240 water bottles at Echo Canyon on 
August 15 and engaged an additional 18 hikers with heat safety information.
