Heat-Related 911 Calls  
      
Phoenix Fire responded to 42 heat-related calls over the reporting period, marking a significant 
increase from the prior week and the highest weekly call volume of the season to date. Nearly 
half of the cases involved residents of age 55 and above. Downtown Phoenix and the I-17 
corridor between Indian School Rd. and Union Hills Dr. had the highest concentration of calls.  
Patient Location and Transport Information:  
• Most common locations: roadways (15), residential (8).  
• 32 patients (76%) were transported for additional care, including eight to Banner 
University and six to Valleywise.  
• Cold water immersion was deployed six times (season total: 15).  
Heat-Related Deaths 
• Eight heat-related deaths have been confirmed in Maricopa County as of 6/6, with three 
of those cases occurring in Phoenix.  
• 92 cases were under investigation in Maricopa County as of 6/6, compared to 64 at the 
same time last year. 
• Of the cases reported countywide to date, 86% occurred outdoors, 63% involved 
substances, and 25% were among people experiencing homelessness.  
• Heat-related death data are updated on the Maricopa County Department of Public 
Health (MCDPH) Heat-Related Illness & Death Surveillance Dashboard

City Program Reports   
• Aviation: Two previously opened heat safety ordinance cases were positively resolved.  
• Heat Response: Nineteen partner organizations in the Heat Relief Network have received 
supplies through the city’s support program in partnership with AllThrive365. Supplies 
distributed include nearly 1,500 shoes, 9,500 electrolyte packets, and over 1,200 hats.   
• Library: Patrons shared appreciation for the availability of water and electrolytes on site and 
the overall welcoming environment.  
• Neighborhood Services: Six new cooling ordinance cases were opened, five were resolved 
with a positive outcome, and two air conditioner replacements were completed.  
• Trailhead Outreach: Community Emergency Response Team volunteers distributed 200 
bottles of water and made 43 educational contacts with hikers at Echo Canyon on June 6.
