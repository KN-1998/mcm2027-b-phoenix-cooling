Heat-Related 911 Calls  
      
Phoenix Fire responded to 13 heat-related calls over the reporting period. While the overall call 
volume was low, the percentage of calls involving adults aged 25–34 was atypically high (5 of 
13 cases, 38%). Of these, at least three were work-related. The percentage of cases involving 
people experiencing homelessness was relatively low (1 of 13, 8%).  
Patient Location and Transport Information:  
• Most common locations: residential (5), businesses (4).  
• 11 patients (85%) were transported for additional care; nine separate hospitals received 
patients.  
• Cold water immersion was not deployed during the reporting week (season total: 9).  
Heat-Related Deaths 
• Four heat-related deaths have been confirmed in Maricopa County as of 5/30, with two 
of those cases occurring in Phoenix.  
• 74 cases were under investigation in Maricopa County as of 5/30, compared to 55 at the 
same time last year. 
• Additional case data will be shared once five cases have been confirmed countywide. 
• Heat-related death data are updated on the Maricopa County Department of Public 
Health (MCDPH) Heat-Related Illness & Death Surveillance Dashboard

City Program Reports   
• Aviation: Two previously opened heat safety ordinance cases were positively resolved.  
• Heat Response: Ten partner organizations in the Heat Relief Network have received 
supplies through the city’s support program in partnership with AllThrive365.  
• Homeless Solutions: Outreach teams engaged with 60 individuals and distributed heat relief 
supplies and information as needed.  
• Innovation: The Chilled Drinking Water Initiative had 14 operating fountains and filling 
stations that dispensed the equivalent of 13,557 single-use plastic bottles.  
• Neighborhood Services: Three new cooling ordinance cases were opened, two were 
resolved with a positive outcome, and two air conditioner replacements were completed.  
• Trailhead Outreach: Community Emergency Response Team volunteers distributed 256 
bottles of water and made 152 educational contacts with hikers at Echo Canyon on May 30.   
• Water Services: Enrolled 46 homes in the low-flow program to ensure continued service.
