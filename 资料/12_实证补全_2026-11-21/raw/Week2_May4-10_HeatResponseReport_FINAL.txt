Summary 
• Triple-digit temperatures returned on May 8 and resulted in increased visits to extended 
hour and overnight heat relief locations.  
• Staff at extended hour and overnight heat relief locations are reporting a high success 
rate in placements to shelter, housing, and social service programs.  
• Communitywide heat illness indicators remain typical for early May.

Heat-Related 911 Calls  
• Phoenix Fire responded to 17 heat-related calls over the reporting period, remaining 
consistent with typical call volumes for early May. Five calls were reported in the 
downtown Phoenix core.  
 
Patient Location and Transport Information:  
• Six patients were in roadway areas, two were in residences, and nine were from other 
areas. 
• 13 patients were transported for additional care, including six to Banner University.  
• Cold water immersion was implemented for one heat illness patient (season total: 2).  
Heat-Related Deaths 
• Two heat-related deaths have been confirmed in Maricopa County as of 5/9. 
• 45 cases were under investigation in Maricopa County as of 5/9, compared to 13 at the 
same time last year. 
• Additional case data will be shared once five cases have been confirmed countywide. 
• Heat-related death data are updated on the Maricopa County Department of Public 
Health (MCDPH) Heat-Related Illness & Death Surveillance Dashboard

City Program Reports   
• Communications: Generated 18 heat safety social media posts that generated 23,247 
impressions and 440 engagements. Supported five media requests on heat programs.  
• Community Assistance Program: Fulfilled nine heat relief transportation requests.  
• Innovation: The Smart Chilled Drinking Water Initiative delivered the equivalent of 16,753 
single-use plastic bottles last week across 13 operating water fountains.  
• Library: Received a note of appreciation from a patron regarding heat relief resources and 
the overall level of safety provided at City libraries.  
• Neighborhood Services: Five new cooling ordinance cases were opened, two cases were 
resolved with positive outcomes, and three air conditioner replacements were completed.  
• Water Services: Added 55 homes to the low-flow program to ensure continued service.
