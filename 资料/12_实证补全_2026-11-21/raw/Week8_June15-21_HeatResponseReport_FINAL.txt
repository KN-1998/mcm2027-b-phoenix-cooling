Heat-Related 911 Calls  
      
Phoenix Fire responded to 70 heat-related calls over the reporting period, marking a new high 
for call volumes this season for the third consecutive week. Over half of patients were adults 
age 25–54. Most calls came from a wide area extending from South Mountain to Camelback 
Road, with the density of calls slightly higher in the central downtown area.  
Patient Location and Transport Information:  
• Most common locations: roadways (22), other business (10).  
• 54 patients (77%) were transported for additional care, including 16 to Banner University 
and 10 to Valleywise.  
• Cold water immersion was deployed 11 times (season total: 38).  
Heat-Related Deaths 
• Eleven heat-related deaths have been confirmed in Maricopa County as of 6/20, with 
five of those cases occurring in Phoenix.  
• 164 cases were under investigation in Maricopa County as of 6/20, compared to 105 at 
the same time last year. 
• Of the cases reported countywide to date, 80% occurred outdoors, 73% involved 
substances, and 27% were among people experiencing homelessness.  
• Heat-related death data are updated on the Maricopa County Department of Public 
Health (MCDPH) Heat-Related Illness & Death Surveillance Dashboard

City Program Reports   
• Communications: 23 social media posts focused on heat response and heat safety 
generated 33,173 impressions and 552 engagements.  
• Community Assistance Program: Provided transportation support for 11 individuals 
accessing heat relief locations.  
• Homeless Solutions: OHS Street Outreach staff engaged 148 individuals, and distributed 
water and heat relief supplies as needed.  Additional heat-related street outreach activities 
are conducted by contractual partners. 
• Neighborhood Services: Twelve new cooling ordinance cases were opened, nine were 
resolved with a positive outcome, and one air conditioner replacement was completed.  
• Trailhead Outreach: Community Emergency Response Team volunteers distributed 240 
bottles of water and made 42 educational contacts with hikers at Echo Canyon on June 20.   
• Water Services: Enrolled 53 new homes in the low-flow program to ensure ongoing service.
